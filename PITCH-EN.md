# Out There – Pitch Foundation

Status: Final version, BadgerBuildFest 2026 · Team: Anna K. & Dennis K. · Madison, Wisconsin

---

## 1. Core Message

**Name:** Out There
**Tagline:** "Find your kind of outside."

**One-sentence pitch:** Out There is a map-based app that understands what you like, when you have time and who you know, and turns that into plans. When the right event doesn't exist yet, **the AI creates it itself.**

**More lines from the app for slides:**
- "Your next reason to head out."
- "It starts in person."
- "More possibilities. Less planning."
- "An app that understands your rhythm and helps you make something of your free time."

**Closing line:** Out There doesn't wait for somebody to organize an event. When enough people want the same thing, the app finds the time, the place and the people — and "we should really do that sometime" becomes "Monday, 4:30 PM, at the lake."

---

## 2. The Problem

- Free time goes unused because planning is too much work: searching, agreeing, finding a date.
- Event platforms only show what is already organized. If fifty people in the same city want to paddle this weekend but nobody sets a time, nothing happens.
- Social apps keep people on their screens instead of getting them out.
- Event listings are generic. They don't know when I'm free, what I like and who's coming along.
- Polls, group chats and scheduling links cost energy. Most ideas die during the coordination. (Observation, not a measured figure.)

## 3. Hard Facts: Why Out There Makes Sense

### 3.1 People spend more time at home
- About **51 minutes less per day outside the home** than in 2019. Analysis of US time-use data from roughly 34,000 respondents, Clemson University, *Journal of the American Planning Association*.
- Time outside the home: **5.5 h (2019) → 4.5 h (2021)**. By 2023 only **11 minutes** had come back.

### 3.2 Less and less time with other people
- American Time Use Survey 2025: **35 minutes per day** socializing and communicating, compared with **41 minutes in 2015**. The share of people socializing on an average day fell from **38 % to 30 %**.
- Screen-based leisure (games, social media) rose over the same period from **25 to 37 minutes** daily.
- Ages 15–24: in-person time with others dropped from **1 hour to 35 minutes** a day.
- US Surgeon General, period 2003–2020:
  - Time with friends **60 → 20 minutes per day**.
  - Time alone **285 → 333 minutes per day**.
  - Ages 15–24: roughly **70 % less** time with friends.

### 3.3 Loneliness is a health risk
In 2023 the US Surgeon General officially declared loneliness an **epidemic**:
- About **half** of US adults report loneliness.
- Lacking social connection raises the risk of premature death as much as **up to 15 cigarettes a day**.
- Heart disease **+29 %**, stroke **+32 %**, dementia in older adults **approx. +50 %**.

### 3.4 Third places are disappearing
- CU Boulder (Jessica Finlay): a **rapid decline** in available third places between 2019 and 2021.
- Majority-Black neighborhoods have fewer libraries; rural areas and communities with lower education levels have fewer civic and social organizations. Churches are closing at an unprecedented rate, and young people say they have nowhere to gather.
- Causes: smartphones, remote work, delivery services, and the closure of libraries, cafés and churches.

### 3.5 The demand is there, the path to it is missing
Eventbrite Social Study, ages 18–35:
- **79 %** want to attend more events in 2026.
- **74 %** consider in-person experiences more important than digital ones.
- **89 %** want events that connect them to their community.
- **79 %** value spontaneity over a perfect plan.
- **52 %** prefer under-the-radar events, **36 %** enjoy hunting for hidden gems.
- **69 %** discover events through their personal network and word of mouth.
- **69 %** want events that combine different interests.
- **55 %** say affordability is very important; the sweet spot is around **$30**.

Additional Eventbrite data:
- **61 %** name word of mouth as their primary way of finding events.
- **92 %** would attend if the event is within an hour's travel.

**Key message:** People want to get out, yet measurably spend more time alone at home. The gap isn't a lack of willingness — it's missing planning, missing places and missing coordination.

### 3.6 Outdoors: many participants, but infrequent
- **183.2 million** Americans (59 % of those aged 6+) are active outdoors, an **all-time record**, nearly **30 million more than in 2019**.
- But **frequency is falling**: engagement is more casual than consistent. The industry wants to turn occasional participants into regular ones. That is exactly what the "?" event delivers.

### 3.7 Advertising is getting more expensive
- Facebook cost per 1,000 impressions: **$5.61 (2023) → $8.96 (2024)**, roughly **+60 %**.
- Cost per click **+10 %** in 2024, cost per lead **+20 %**.
- **60 %** of organizers planned to raise ticket prices because of rising costs.

### 3.8 Vacancy at a record high
- Office vacancy across 79 US markets: **21 % in Q1 2026, a record**. Before 2020 it stood at roughly 16–17 %.

### 3.9 Why now? (one slide)
- **The problem is official:** loneliness is classified as an epidemic, with a health risk comparable to up to 15 cigarettes a day.
- **The behavior is measurable:** 51 minutes less outside the home, time with friends down from 60 to 20 minutes a day.
- **The demand is there:** 79 % want more events, 89 % want community connection.
- **Advertising is getting more expensive:** around +60 % in a single year.
- **The spaces exist:** 21 % office vacancy.
- **The technology is ready:** AI with web search can find events, plan out hobbies and coordinate groups. Our prototype shows it.

## 4. The Solution

**A map instead of a feed:** calm, without shops and commercial listings, showing only events that fit you. On top of it sits a quiet, learning layer:

- **It learns in the background** from saves, joins and skips, time of day, distance and — optionally — your calendar.
- **It only asks when it helps:** small cards with a one-tap answer.
- **It explains every recommendation** ("Why this").
- **It creates its own events** when several people want the same thing, and finds the best time.

People meet in person: no public profiles, no browsing strangers, no contact details in the app.

---

## 5. USP: The AI Creates Its Own Events (the "?")

Out There doesn't just display events — it **lets events come into being**.

### 5.1 How it works
1. The AI recognizes that several connections and people nearby want the same thing, for example to get out on the water. There is no organizer and no time yet.
2. A **dashed "?" pin** appears on the map.
3. Anyone who wants in taps "I'm in" and picks their time window.
4. **24 hours before**, Out There calculates the window where the most people are free, notifies everyone, and turns the "?" into a **confirmed event pin**.

### 5.2 Demo example
- **Event:** "Lake Mendota Canoe & Paddle — Community Gathering"
- **Location:** Hoofer Outdoor Club · Memorial Union Terrace, 800 Langdon St, Madison
- **Date:** Monday, September 28
- **Cost:** canoe rental approx. $15
- **Time windows and votes:** Morning 9–11 AM (3), Afternoon 2–4 PM (4), Late afternoon 4–6 PM (7), Anytime (2), for a total of **16 people interested**
- **Result:** "Confirmed · Monday, 4:30 PM". "Anytime" votes count toward every window.
- **Notification:** "It's on — canoe & paddle, Monday 4:30 PM at Hoofers. See you on the water."
- **Why this:** "2 connections want to paddle" · "You like being outside" · "17 people interested" · "11 min away"
- **Visibility:** only for users interested in Outdoors or Movement, or with the "Move" chip active.

### 5.3 How the AI "works out" a hobby (canoe example)
1. **Detect demand:** interest in outdoors or paddling, free time on Monday.
2. **What does the hobby need?** A body of water, boats or a rental, life jackets, skill level, cost.
3. **Where does it fit?** A nearby lake with a rental and good access — in the demo, Lake Mendota with the Hoofers, approx. 11 minutes away, around $15.
4. **When does it fit?** Weather (temperature, rain) and the participants' time windows.
5. **Form the date:** "?" event, vote, lock-in 24 hours before, notification to everyone.

**Implemented:**
- Grouping interests
- Weather check (Open-Meteo)
- Voting on time windows and locking in the date (tested)
- Visibility only for matching interests
- Why this

**Next:** automatically finding the location and rental via AI web search. The building block already exists in the event scraper. In the demo the location and rental are hardcoded.

### 5.4 Second stage of the same principle: community meetups from interests
- The Event-Creator agent groups interests by activity and free time window, checks the weather, and proposes meetups — for example "11 people nearby want to do yoga outside on Saturday morning."
- Threshold in the prototype: **9 people**. Once you join and the threshold is met, the meetup appears as a red community pin.
- Activities: Group run, Yoga in the park, Photo walk, Board game night, Founder coffee, Pickup soccer, Sketch & chill.
- Time windows: Saturday morning, Saturday evening, Sunday morning, Sunday afternoon, Wednesday evening.
- 90 simulated people, at most one proposal per activity, up to 5 in total.
- Claude (fast model) writes the invitation texts; without an API key, templates take over.

**Why this is strong:** Other platforms need an organizer. Out There turns demand directly into supply and runs the coordination automatically.

## 6. How Events Reach the Map (5 Sources)

| Pin | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | Partner (sponsored) | Organizers such as ticket providers or agencies pay for visibility, always labelled ("Sponsored · Partner", "Partner event"). |
| Navy | Verified hosts | Events from trusted organizers. Ratings build reputation, which lifts them in "For You". |
| Green | Found by AI on the web | Real events at your location and in the selected time range. |
| Red | Community meetup | Created by the AI from matching interests. |
| "?" dashed | Time open | The AI coordinates the time by vote (USP). |

## 7. The AI Event Scraper (real events, live)

- Location and day default to the current position and today's date. OpenStreetMap Nominatim resolves the city.
- Our **own scraper prompt** goes to Claude with web search: "You are an event scraper for the given region / city…" (in the repo at `prompts/event-scraper.md`).
- **Result:** the top 20 events as JSON with coordinates, name, organizer, time, cost, capacity, registration yes/no, link and hashtags, plus category and Social/Professional.
- **Validation:** date within range, at most approx. 60 km away, http(s) links only. Missing coordinates are derived from the address.
- **Time range:** the timeline decides day, week (Mon–Sun) or month, top 20 each.
- **Cost control:** the search only starts once you release the scrubber. Each range is requested only once and **cached for 12 hours**. Maximum **8 web searches** per request, duration approx. 20–60 seconds.
- **Security:** web content is neutralized before display. Note in the sheet: "Details were collected by AI from public web pages. Check the event page before you go."

## 8. The Learning Layer ("Observe first. Ask only when useful.")

### 8.1 Onboarding in 10 seconds
- One screen: "What gets you out there?"
- 10 chips: Movement, Food, Music, Art + Design, Outdoors, Wellness, Culture, Networking, Community, Something new.
- "Start exploring", and below it: "That's enough for now. Out There learns what works for you as you go."

### 8.2 The map stays home
- "Good evening, Anna" and "What are we feeling?" with 4 chips: **Move, Meet people, Food, Something random**. One tap filters the map instantly.
- The greeting stays in place while you pan the map; a light gradient from the top keeps it legible.

### 8.3 Contextual moments (only ever one, answered with a tap)
- "Free today?" → Until 2 / All day / Not today
- "Want more like this?" → after the first save
- "You've got some time tonight. I found 4 things that fit your usual Saturday vibe." → Show me
- "Seems like outdoor plans are your kind of thing. Want me to put them first while the weather is good?"
- "Want me to work around your schedule?" → Connect calendar / Maybe later, with the privacy line: "Out There can use your free/busy windows without displaying your event details."
- **Concierge moment:**
  - "You have a window Saturday afternoon. 4:00–7:00 PM · Good weather · Sarah is free too. I found something you might actually like."
  - Suggestion: Outdoor Ceramics Pop-Up, 5 PM, approx. 10 minutes away, $18.
  - Buttons: "Make a plan" / "Show me something else".

### 8.4 Why this + Not for me
- Every card briefly explains why it appears, for example "You like movement", "12 min away", "Fits your evening", "2 connections interested", "Partner event".
- "Not for me" hides the event and lowers similar events in the ranking.

### 8.5 "What Out There knows" (transparency instead of a dashboard)
- Personalization on or off with one switch.
- Learned signals in everyday language, each one editable or removable:
  - "Likes movement + art + design"
  - "Prefers things nearby"
  - "Usually looks for plans in the evening"
  - "Usually chooses events under $40"
  - "Usually free on weekend mornings"
  - "Often makes spontaneous plans"
  - "Fewer sport events"
- Sources: calendar (free/busy only), location (approximate), connections.
- Tone rule: never "we noticed / we tracked / we know", but "Want more like this?", "Free tonight?", "Here's something that fits."

### 8.6 Ask Out There (not a giant chatbot)
- Suggestions: "I have 3 hours free." · "Sarah and I have Saturday afternoon free." · "Something outdoors under $30." · "Get me out of the house."
- Understands time windows, budget, outdoor preferences and friends mentioned by name.
- Claude picks the 3 best events and answers in one sentence, for example "I found three things that fit both of you."
- Every answer leads to an action: View & join, Save, Invite Sarah, Add to calendar.
- Without an API key, or after 12 seconds with no answer, the app takes over with its own logic.

### 8.7 The AI visibly evolves
- **Day 1:** knows a few interests.
- **Week 1:** knows event types, distance and typical times.
- **Later:** calendar connected, spots free windows and proactively suggests plans.

## 9. Interaction & Design

- **Launch screen:** approx. 3 seconds of the Out There figure with "out there", then a soft transition into the map.
- **Timeline:**
  - Thumb-scroll through the days. Swipe up for the week, again for the month, down to go back.
  - Spring animation; dots show where something is happening.
  - Compact, collapsible, in the marker color, with a strong gradient toward the bottom.
- **Pill navigation**, floating: Map, Events, Connections, AI. The profile is reached via the avatar in the top right.
- **Map:** calm vector map without shops and POIs, water and parks keep their color, nearby pins are clustered.
- **Event sheet:** preview and full view, swipe between events, Join/Save, share, directions, "Sarah + 1 connection going".
- **Filters in the profile:** Social/Professional, categories, distance (≤ 15/25 minutes), time of day, budget (Free, ≤ $20, ≤ $40), "With my connections".
- **No empty placeholders:** quiet hints such as "Quiet day · see the whole week" or "3 more just outside this view".
- **My Events:** upcoming plans as tickets, saved items, private history.
- **Connections:** created only after a real meeting ("It starts in person. No public profiles. No people browsing.").

## 10. Business Model

**Implemented in the prototype:**
- Paid partner placement with a limited, labelled advantage (+1.5 in the score, "Partner event" in Why this). Money never outranks genuine relevance.

**Cost advantage for organizers** (our argument, not yet measured):
- **Less waste:** partner events only reach users whose interests, distance and free time fit.
- **Demand before the event:** with a "?" event, providers such as a canoe rental know in advance how many people are coming and when. That lowers the risk of empty slots and saves the advertising needed to fill them initially.
- **Digital word of mouth:** 61–69 % discover events through their networks. Out There shows which connections are going.
- **Low-cost entry:** verified hosts need no ad budget; reputation lifts them organically.

**Possible expansions (proposals):**
- Ticket or booking commission
- Premium package for hosts with reach and analytics
- Partnerships with venues and rental providers that gain a guaranteed flow of guests through "?" events
- Offerings for cities and space providers (see roadmap)
- **No sharing of personal data** — that is part of the brand.

## 11. For-You Ranking (logic)

**Base score:**
- Interest match: +3
- Friends attending: +1.5 per person, max. 2
- Host reputation: rating − 3
- Partner: +1.5
- Community event: +1
- Distance: up to −3
- Already over: −10

**Learning layer on top:**
- "More like this": +1.5
- "Not for me": −2.5 per category
- Outdoor priority: +2
- Above the usual budget: −2
- Too far away: −1.5
- Evenings: +0.8
- Calendar busy: −3, free: +1
- Today's availability ("Until 2", "All day", "Not today") shifts the ranking accordingly.

## 12. Technology

- **Frontend:** plain JavaScript (ES modules), no framework and no build step. Web app in fullscreen via "Add to Home Screen".
- **Map:** Leaflet with a MapLibre vector map (OpenFreeMap), POIs filtered out.
- **Server:** Node.js without a framework, reachable on the local network via self-signed HTTPS so the phone will share its location.
- **AI:** Claude Sonnet 5 with web search for the scraper, Claude Haiku 4.5 for invitation texts and Ask Out There.
- **APIs:** Nominatim (geocoding), Open-Meteo (weather).
- **Endpoints:** `/api/events`, `/api/proposals`, `/api/ask`, `/api/status`.
- **Quality:** **24 automated tests**, covering time filters, privacy, ranking, request parsing, "?" consensus and protection against harmful content.
- **Security:** keys stay on the server and never in the repo, web content is neutralized, only the app folders are served.
- **Development:** built with AI support (Claude, Claude Code in VS Code), versioned on GitHub.

## 13. Privacy & Values

- No public profiles, no browsing strangers, no contact details in the app.
- Connections only after real meetings. You see friends only if they share their attendance.
- Calendar optional and only once the value is clear. Only free/busy is used.
- Everything learned is visible, editable and removable; personalization can be switched off entirely.
- Sponsored content is always labelled.
- Guiding principle: the AI should save time, not create another thing to manage.

## 14. Roadmap

### 14.1 Next steps
- Real user intents instead of simulated people.
- Automatic search for location and rental for "?" events via AI web search.
- Push notifications for the 24-hour confirmation.
- Real calendar integration (Google, Apple, Outlook), free/busy only.
- Connections via QR code when meeting in person.
- Post-event ratings feeding host reputation.
- Partner portal for sponsored events.
- Native app (e.g. via Capacitor) and expansion beyond Madison.

### 14.2 Vision: "Places find people" (bringing forgotten spaces back to life)
Today Out There brings people to events. In the future the app brings **unused places and the right people together**.

**How it works:**
1. **List places:** cities, owners, clubs, universities or organizer cohorts enter free rooms and spaces — empty storefronts, unused office floors, community halls, gyms in the evening or rooftops. Details: size, capacity, equipment, accessibility, hours, cost, rules.
2. **Detect demand:** for example, 25 people in a neighborhood want board games, ceramics, dance or a repair café.
3. **Match:** if the space fits and there is enough interest, the AI proposes a "?" event at that location, with a vote on the time windows.
4. **Lock in the date:** the date is fixed 24 hours before. The space provider knows the headcount in advance.
5. **Repeat:** a successful date turns into a regular group, and the place becomes a new gathering spot.

**Why this makes sense:**
- 21 % office vacancy while gathering places are disappearing.
- Temporary use of vacant buildings ("meanwhile use") saves CO₂, energy and water compared with demolition and new construction, creates economic momentum, makes room for youth and social programs, and strengthens identification with the neighborhood. Examples: the UK's "Open Doors" scheme for empty storefronts, Scotland's Community Empowerment Act 2015.

**Value for every side:**
- **People:** new places nearby, cheap or free.
- **Owners and cities:** activity instead of vacancy, plus anonymized, aggregated signals about demand in the neighborhood — never personal data.
- **Out There:** new supply without traditional organizers, and possible partnerships with cities and property owners.

**Steps:**
1. Place profiles and an intake form for space providers.
2. AI matching of activity and space requirements.
3. "?" events at places with a locked-in date.
4. Recurring groups and an overview for cities.

## 17. Honest Limits of the Prototype

- The people behind meetups and votes are simulated.
- Payments, host verification, ratings and the calendar integration are mocked.
- The confirmed state of the "?" event is shown via a prototype switch. The consensus logic is real and tested.
- The location and rental for the canoe event are hardcoded; automatic search is on the roadmap.
- The AI calls were tested with simulated responses. A real run needs an API key.
- There is no solid study on how often group plans fall through. Present that point as an observation.
